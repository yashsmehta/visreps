# Extended Data Fig. 7b: THINGS behavioural alignment is stable across random training seeds

**Extended Data Fig. 7b | Per-seed THINGS behavioural alignment scores cluster tightly around their condition means.**
Individual per-seed scores for every CLIP-PCA coarse condition (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 classes; `pca_labels_folder LIKE '%clip%'`) and for the 1,000-class supervised baseline (`pca_labels_folder = 'imagenet1k'`), drawn with distinct markers — circle (seed 1), square (seed 2), triangle (seed 3) — and a small multiplicative jitter in log-space per seed to prevent overplotting. Coarse markers are dark blue (CLIP); 1,000-way markers at the broken-axis 1,000 position are orange. Each score is the per-seed best-layer RSA score returned by `get_best_layer_results` on the 80 % concept-level THINGS evaluation split (`subject_idx = "N/A"`, so the mean-across-subjects aggregation reduces to a single value). A short dashed horizontal tick at each cfg shows the mean of the three seed scores; the untrained-network baseline (epoch 0) is a grey dashed horizontal line labelled "Untrained". The y-limits are force-matched to Figure 4b via `_precompute_fig4_behavioral_ylim`, and the broken x-axis matches main Fig. 4b.

No confidence intervals or error bars are drawn in this panel — only the raw per-seed scores and their across-seed mean. Across all granularity levels the three seeds cluster closely around the dashed mean line, visibly tighter than the coarse-vs-1,000-class contrast in main Fig. 4b, indicating that training stochasticity (random initialisation, data shuffling, dropout) is not a meaningful driver of the reported behavioural coarseness effect.
