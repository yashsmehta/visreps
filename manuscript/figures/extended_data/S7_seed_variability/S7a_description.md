# Extended Data Fig. 7a: Neural alignment is stable across random training seeds

**Extended Data Fig. 7a | Per-seed neural alignment scores cluster tightly around their condition means.**
Individual per-seed scores for every coarse condition and the 1,000-class baseline. The trained model is the same AlexNet-style CustomCNN throughout. For each CLIP-PCA coarse condition (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 classes; `pca_labels_folder LIKE '%clip%'`) and for the standard 1,000-class supervised baseline (`pca_labels_folder = 'imagenet1k'`), the three seeds (1, 2, 3) are drawn as separate markers — circle (seed 1), square (seed 2), triangle (seed 3) — with a small multiplicative jitter in log-space per seed to prevent overplotting. Coarse markers are dark blue (CLIP); the 1,000-way markers at the broken-axis 1,000 position are orange. Each score is the per-seed **mean across subjects** of the subject-wise best-layer score, obtained from `get_best_layer_results` in `S7_seed_variability.py`. A short dashed horizontal tick at each cfg shows the mean of the three seed scores; the untrained-network baseline (epoch 0, mean across subjects) is a grey dashed horizontal line, labelled "Untrained" on the bottom row. Broken x-axis and y-limits are force-matched to the corresponding panels of main Fig. 3 via `_precompute_fig3_ylims`.
**a**, Macaque TVSD V1. **b**, Macaque TVSD IT. **c**, Human NSD early visual stream. **d**, Human NSD ventral visual stream.
No confidence intervals or error bars are drawn in this panel — only the raw per-seed means and their across-seed mean. Inter-seed scatter is therefore read directly off the markers: across all conditions and benchmarks the three seeds cluster closely around the dashed mean line, visibly tighter than the 1,000-way-versus-coarse contrasts in the corresponding panels of main Fig. 3, indicating that training stochasticity (random initialisation, data shuffling, dropout) is not a meaningful driver of the reported alignment patterns.
