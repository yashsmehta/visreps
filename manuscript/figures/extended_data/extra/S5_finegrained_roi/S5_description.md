# Supplementary Figure 5: Fine-grained NSD ROI decomposition

**Supplementary Fig. 5 | Fine-grained decomposition of the NSD ventral hierarchy reveals a gradient from retinotopic to category-selective cortex.**
Raw representational similarity (Spearman *ρ*) as a function of supervisory granularity (2–64 classes, log₂ scale) for six individual NSD regions of interest spanning the full ventral hierarchy from retinotopic to category-selective cortex. Two PCA label sources are overlaid per panel: AlexNet (light blue circles) and CLIP ViT-L/14 (dark blue squares). The 1,000-class supervised baseline is marked by an orange diamond and the untrained-network baseline by a grey dashed line. Broken x-axis and jittered markers match main Fig. 3.
**a**, V1. **b**, V2. **c**, V3. **d**, hV4. **e**, fusiform face area (FFA). **f**, parahippocampal place area (PPA). V1, V2 and V3 (**a**–**c**) each individually exhibit the characteristic flat coarseness profile, confirming that early-visual coarseness independence holds at the level of individual retinotopic maps and not only in aggregate. The most pronounced granularity dependence appears in category-selective cortex (**e**, **f**), consistent with the known functional specialisation of FFA and PPA for faces, objects and scene categories; hV4 (**d**) occupies an intermediate position. Main Fig. 3 collapses V1–V3 into the early visual stream and hV4 together with the category-selective areas into the ventral visual stream; this decomposition reveals that the ventral-stream coarseness effect reported there is driven primarily by category-selective cortex rather than by intermediate visual areas.
Error bars denote 95% bootstrap confidence intervals aggregated across 8 NSD subjects and three independently trained networks per condition.
