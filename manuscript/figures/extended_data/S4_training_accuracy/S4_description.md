# Extended Data Fig. 4: Training convergence and classification accuracy do not predict alignment

**Extended Data Fig. 4 | All models successfully learn their classification tasks, but classification accuracy does not predict brain or behavioural alignment.**
Final test-set classification accuracy (epoch 20, mean ± SEM across three independently trained networks) as a function of supervisory granularity (2–64 classes, log₂ scale) for all four PCA label sources: AlexNet (light blue circles), CLIP ViT-L/14 (dark blue squares), supervised ViT-L/16 (crimson triangles) and DINOv3 (teal pentagons). The 1,000-class supervised baseline (orange bar), shared across all sources, is shown to the right of a broken x-axis; marker positions are jittered to reduce overplotting.
Accuracy decreases monotonically with the number of classes for every label source, as expected. Crucially, at matched granularity there are large accuracy differences across PCA source models: AlexNet-derived labels produce the easiest classification tasks (~96% at 2-way, ~74% at 64-way), whereas DINOv3-derived labels produce the hardest (~78% at 2-way, ~40% at 64-way). Despite these substantial accuracy differences, all four sources yield comparable brain and behavioural alignment (Extended Data Fig. 1). This double dissociation — classification accuracy varies with label source while alignment does not — rules out classification accuracy as the driver of alignment quality and demonstrates that it is the *granularity* of the supervisory signal, not task difficulty or task performance, that determines representational alignment with biological vision.
