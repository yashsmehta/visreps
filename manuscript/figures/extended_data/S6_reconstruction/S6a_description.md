# Extended Data Fig. 6a: Neural alignment persists across representational dimensionality

**Extended Data Fig. 6a | The coarse-model advantage over the 1,000-class baseline in visual cortex persists across the full range of retained representational dimensions.**
Representational similarity (Spearman *ρ*) as a function of the number of top principal components retained from the best-layer activations (*k* = 1, 2, …, 50), for the best coarse model (blue) and the 1,000-class supervised baseline (orange). The untrained-network baseline is shown as a grey dotted line; shaded bands denote 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. The best coarse model is selected per benchmark from training-set performance: 64-class AlexNet for TVSD V1, TVSD IT and NSD early visual stream, and 16-class CLIP for NSD ventral visual stream.
**a**, Macaque TVSD V1. **b**, Macaque TVSD IT. **c**, Human NSD early visual stream. **d**, Human NSD ventral visual stream. Both the coarse and the 1,000-class curves plateau rapidly — typically by *k* ≈ 10–20 PCs — and the coarse model's advantage over the 1,000-class baseline is maintained across the full range of retained dimensions in every panel. The coarse model's superior neural alignment is therefore not confined to a handful of leading components but persists as the representational space expands toward its full rank, ruling out a dimensionality-matching artifact and indicating that the structural correspondence with neural responses is genuine.
