# Extended Data Fig. 5: Interpretable visual–semantic structure of the PCA coarse-graining axes

**Extended Data Fig. 5 | The principal components used for coarse-graining capture interpretable visual–semantic axes.**
For each of two PCA source models — AlexNet (left half) and CLIP ViT-L/14 (right half) — the five most-activating and five least-activating ImageNet training images are shown along principal components PC1 through PC6, with the variance explained (%) annotated for each component. These PCs define the binary splits used to generate coarse labels by recursive median splits: PC1 produces the 2-class partition, PC1 together with PC2 produces the 4-class partition, and so on up to 64 classes with PC1–PC6.
For AlexNet, PC1 primarily separates broadly achromatic, manufactured objects from colourful natural scenes — a largely low-level visual distinction that nonetheless tracks a meaningful perceptual boundary; later components capture further texture- and colour-based contrasts. For CLIP the axes are considerably more semantically structured: PC1 cleanly separates animals and natural scenes from artifacts and text, and later PCs (2–6) capture progressively finer object- and scene-category distinctions. Despite these substantial differences in what the axes encode, models trained on labels from both sources achieve comparable brain and behavioural alignment (Extended Data Fig. 1), indicating that the *granularity* of the partitioning — how many categories it produces — matters more than the particular semantic content along which images are divided. All images are drawn from the ImageNet training set.
