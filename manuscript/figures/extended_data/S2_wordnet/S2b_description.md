# Extended Data Fig. 2b: THINGS behavioural alignment with WordNet-derived coarse labels

**Extended Data Fig. 2b | WordNet-derived coarse labels match or exceed the 1,000-class baseline on human behavioural similarity.**
Representational similarity (Spearman *ρ*) between model FC1 activations and the THINGS behavioural similarity embedding, as a function of supervisory granularity, for labels obtained by hierarchical agglomerative clustering of the 1,000 ImageNet synsets under Wu–Palmer semantic similarity (2, 3, 4, 10, 20 and 57 classes; forest-green hexagons). The 1,000-class supervised baseline is marked by an orange diamond and the untrained-network baseline by a grey dashed line. Broken x-axis and jittered markers match main Fig. 4.
WordNet-derived coarse models match or substantially exceed the 1,000-class baseline at every granularity level, mirroring the PCA-based behavioural result in main Fig. 4 and Extended Data Fig. 1b. The convergence across two fundamentally different label-generation procedures — one grounded in learned visual feature statistics, the other in purely lexical-semantic taxonomy — provides strong evidence that the coarseness advantage reflects a genuine property of how coarse category boundaries shape learned representations, rather than an artifact of any particular method for constructing those boundaries.
Error bars denote 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (1,000 iterations) aggregated across three independently trained networks per condition, computed on the 80% concept-level THINGS evaluation split.
