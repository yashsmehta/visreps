# Supplementary Figure 1a: Neural alignment across all four PCA label sources

**Supplementary Fig. 1a | The coarseness–alignment relationship in visual cortex is independent of the pretrained model used to define the labels.**
Raw representational similarity (Spearman *ρ*) as a function of supervisory granularity (2–64 classes, log₂ scale), with four PCA label sources overlaid per panel: AlexNet (light blue circles), CLIP ViT-L/14 (dark blue squares), a supervised ViT-L/16 (crimson triangles) and DINOv3 (teal pentagons). The 1,000-class supervised baseline is marked by an orange diamond and the untrained-network baseline by a grey dashed line. The x-axis is broken before the 1,000-class position and marker positions are jittered to reduce overplotting; layout and visual style match main Fig. 3.
**a**, Macaque TVSD V1. **b**, Macaque TVSD IT. **c**, Human NSD early visual stream. **d**, Human NSD ventral visual stream. Early visual regions (**a**, **c**) are flat across granularity levels for all four label sources, while higher visual regions (**b**, **d**) show a gradual increase that converges near or above the 1,000-class baseline by 32–64 classes. The qualitative pattern is therefore the same across four pretrained models spanning very different architectures and training objectives — a CNN trained on ImageNet classification (AlexNet), a contrastively trained vision–language transformer (CLIP), a supervised transformer (ViT-L/16) and a self-supervised transformer (DINOv3) — indicating that what matters is the granularity of the partitioning, not the specific geometry of the model used to define the label boundaries.
Error bars denote 95% bootstrap confidence intervals aggregated across subjects (or monkeys) and three independently trained networks per condition.
